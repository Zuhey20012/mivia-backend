# Fundraising plan: from first euro to a possible €400 million

Written 30 Oct 2026. This is planning guidance, not investment, legal or tax advice. Use a lawyer for every round, and an accountant for the cap table.

## The honest starting point
- **€400 million is late-stage money.** In Europe only a small number of companies a year raise that much in total, and they do it over 6–10 years and several rounds, after proving a large, profitable market position. For comparison, Wolt raised about US$530 million in one round in January 2021, years after launching and while already operating in many countries.
- **Malvoya today:** the product is built, but there is no company, no revenue, no shops and no customers. **Investors pay for proof** (traction, unit economics, team), not for code alone.
- **So the plan is a ladder:** each round buys the proof that unlocks the next, larger round. Reaching about €400 m in total by around 2032 is possible only if every step below is hit.

Each round typically sells **15–25 %** of the company. Raising in steps keeps more ownership for you than raising early at a low price.

## What investors check at every round
1. **Team:** a strong co-founder or early hires in operations and technology. A solo founder raising large rounds is rare, so hire or partner early.
2. **Traction:** orders, GMV, retention, shop and courier numbers, growth week by week, all from the admin analytics (already built).
3. **Unit economics:** contribution per order and payback on marketing (the operating plan calculator).
4. **Market:** fashion and beauty spending (about €5.4 bn a year in Finland alone, see the MVP report) and how Malvoya wins a share of it.
5. **Defensibility:** local shop network, Drops content, data on sizes and shades, brand.
6. **Clean company:** Oy with a clean cap table, IP owned by the company (the code is assigned to the Oy), signed shareholder agreement, contracts, GDPR, bookkeeping.

## What must not happen
- Don't promise investors numbers that haven't happened. Show the beta's real metrics, even small ones.
- Don't raise large money before the unit economics work: it multiplies losses.
- Don't sign anything without a lawyer, especially on valuation caps, liquidation preferences and control rights.
