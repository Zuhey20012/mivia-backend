# 04 — Keeping control while raising money

> **Not legal, investment or tax advice.** Planning material for a Finnish lawyer and accountant, written 3–5 November 2026. Statute references are to the Limited Liability Companies Act 624/2006 ("OYL", Finlex consolidated version `fin@20240652`, read 3 Nov 2026). Market practice described here ("investors usually…") is general guidance, not data. Every round needs a lawyer on the founder's side.

## 1. The three layers of control

| Layer | Document | What it controls | Who can change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Votes** | Articles of association (02) | Who elects the board; qualified-majority decisions | General meeting: 2/3 of votes **and** of shares represented (OYL ch. 5 §27), plus the A-holder approval in clause 13 of the draft |
| **Contract** | Shareholders' agreement (osakassopimus) | Vetoes, board seats, leaver terms, drag/tag, transfer limits, information rights | All parties to it (by its own amendment clause) |
| **People** | Board and management | Day-to-day decisions, hiring, budgets | General meeting elects the board; the board appoints the managing director |

The founder keeps control when all three line up: A-share votes elect a founder-majority board, the shareholders' agreement lists only a **short** set of investor vetoes, and the founder is managing director.

## 2. Tools investors will discuss, and the founder's position

### 2.1 Founder share classes
- Covered in 02 (A = 10 votes, B = 1 vote, A converts on transfer).
- **Expect:** a request for a sunset (A converts after N years, at IPO, or if the founder leaves), or for one share one vote at Series A.
- **Push for:** conversion only on transfer or on a **bad-leaver** exit; any time-based sunset as long as possible (for example 7–10 years) or tied to an IPO.

### 2.2 Board composition
- **Pre-seed:** founder alone (+ deputy) or founder + one independent adviser.
- **Seed:** typical 3 seats: founder, founder-nominated (or independent agreed by both), one investor seat. A 3-member board needs no deputy (OYL ch. 6 §8).
- **Series A:** often 5 seats: 2 founder/management, 2 investors, 1 independent agreed by both.
- **Push for:** the founder side naming the majority while the founder holds A-share vote control; the chair elected by the general meeting (draft clause 5); an **observer** seat instead of a full seat for small investors.
- Board members are liable for breaching the Companies Act or the articles (OYL ch. 22 §1): investors appointing members will want D&O insurance (08).

### 2.3 Reserved matters (vetoes)
Decisions that need investor approval even if the founder has the votes.
- **Accept (normal):** changes to share rights or the articles that hurt the investor class; new share classes ranking ahead of them; liquidation, merger or sale of the company; directed share issues outside an agreed option pool; related-party deals with the founder; borrowing above a set limit; changing the business fundamentally.
- **Push back on:** annual budget approval, every hire above a low salary, every contract above a low amount, choice of managing director, product decisions. These turn investors into managers.
- Draft clause 13 gives the founder the mirror veto in the articles.

### 2.4 Vesting and leaver terms (and why investors ask)
- **Why:** investors pay for the team. If a founder leaves after six months with all their shares, the company is left with a large passive owner and no one to do the work. Vesting means the founder's shares "earn in" over time; unvested shares can be bought back if they leave.
- **Typical:** 4 years, 1-year cliff, monthly or quarterly after. Finnish practice implements this contractually: the shareholders' agreement gives the company or the other shareholders a right to buy the leaver's unvested shares (and sometimes vested ones) at a set price.
- **Good leaver** (illness, death, dismissal without cause): keeps vested shares; unvested bought at fair value. **Bad leaver** (resigns early, serious breach, competing): unvested and sometimes vested bought at the lower of cost and fair value.
- **Push for:** credit for time already worked (the code exists since 2025–2026: ask for 25–50 % vested at signing); **acceleration** on a sale of the company (double trigger: sale + losing the role); a narrow, objectively defined bad-leaver definition; no bad-leaver treatment for simply being removed from the managing director role by the board.
- **Never accept** vesting on shares the founder bought with their own money without a price that returns at least what was paid.

### 2.5 Pre-emption rights
- By law each shareholder may subscribe for new shares in proportion to their holding (OYL ch. 9 §3); a directed issue needs a qualified majority and a weighty financial reason (ch. 9 §4, ch. 5 §27).
- Investors usually want pre-emption in the agreement too, often including a **pro rata right** in future rounds. **Accept**: it protects the founder as well. Keep an exception for the option pool and for strategic issues approved by the board.

### 2.6 Drag-along and tag-along
- **Drag-along** (myötämyyntivelvollisuus): if holders of a set majority accept an offer for the whole company, everyone else must sell on the same terms. **Push for:** a high threshold that cannot be reached without the founder (for example 75 % of all shares and the A-holder majority), a minimum price or a minimum multiple, no drag in the first years, and the same price per share for everyone (after preferences).
- **Tag-along** (myötämyyntioikeus): if a shareholder sells control, the others may sell on the same terms. **Accept:** it protects minority holders, including the founder later.

### 2.7 Anti-dilution
- Protects investors if a later round is priced lower ("down round") by giving them more shares.
- **Accept:** broad-based weighted average.
- **Refuse:** full ratchet (resets their price to the new low price; it can wipe out the founder).

### 2.8 Liquidation preference basics
- Decides who is paid first when the company is sold or wound up.
- **1× non-participating** (standard, accept): the investor gets either their money back **or** their percentage of the proceeds, whichever is more.
- **Participating** (push back): money back **and** their percentage of the rest ("double dip").
- **Multiples above 1×** (refuse at early stages).
- In Finland preferences are usually implemented in the shareholders' agreement as an agreed distribution of sale proceeds, or through a preferred share class in the articles. Ask the lawyer which, because the articles' equal-rights default (OYL ch. 1 §7, ch. 3 §1) applies unless the articles create the class.

### 2.9 Option pools
- A pool of B shares (or option rights to them, OYL ch. 10) reserved for employees.
- **Typical:** 5–15 % of the company after the round.
- **Push for:** the pool created **after** the round's price is set (so it dilutes everyone, not only the founder; investors often want it "pre-money"); grants decided by the board within an authorisation; standard vesting for employees.
- Employee options have tax consequences in Finland (accountant).

## 3. Founder-friendly term-sheet checklist

| Term | Accept | Push back | Refuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| Valuation / price | A fair, explained pre-money | Pool counted pre-money | "We'll set the valuation later" with a low cap and no discount |
| Instrument | Shares; or a convertible loan / SAFE-style note with a reasonable cap and discount | Interest-bearing loan with personal guarantee | Personal guarantees by the founder |
| Share class | Investor B or preferred shares; A shares stay with the founder | Sunset on A shares | Removal of A votes at the first round |
| Liquidation preference | 1× non-participating | Participating with a cap | > 1×, uncapped participation |
| Anti-dilution | Broad-based weighted average | Narrow-based | Full ratchet |
| Board | Founder majority; one investor seat or observer | Investor-majority after Series A | Investor majority at pre-seed or seed |
| Reserved matters | Short list of structural vetoes (§2.3) | Operational vetoes | Veto on hiring/firing the founder as managing director without cause |
| Vesting | 4 years, credit for past work, double-trigger acceleration | No credit for past work | Re-vesting of shares already earned with no price protection |
| Leaver | Narrow bad-leaver; fair value for good leavers | Broad bad-leaver | Bad leaver = any removal by the board |
| Drag-along | High threshold incl. founder, price floor | Low threshold after 5 years | Drag without the founder at any price |
| Tag-along | Yes | — | — |
| Pre-emption / pro rata | Yes | Super pro rata | — |
| Information rights | Monthly or quarterly KPIs, annual accounts | Weekly reporting | Access to personal data of users (GDPR) |
| Non-compete for the founder | While a shareholder + 12 months, Finland/EU, the marketplace business | 24 months | Worldwide, all of e-commerce, unlimited time |
| Exclusivity / no-shop | 30–45 days after a signed term sheet | 60 days | Before a term sheet |
| Costs | Company pays investor's reasonable legal fees with a cap | No cap | — |
| Redemption / put right | — | — | Investor right to force the company to buy them back |

## 4. Shareholders' agreement (osakassopimus) — template outline

> For the lawyer to draft. Parties: all shareholders and the company. Language: Finnish or English, with Finnish law and a Finnish forum or arbitration.

1. **Parties, background, definitions.**
2. **Purpose and business plan** (marketplace in Finland first; changes need a reserved-matter approval).
3. **Precedence:** between the shareholders the agreement prevails; the articles must not conflict; all parties vote to amend the articles if needed.
4. **Board:** number of seats, who nominates which seat, the chair (general meeting, founder side), observer rights, quorum, D&O insurance, board fees.
5. **Management:** the founder as managing director; how the managing director can be replaced (only for cause, or with founder consent while A votes are in place).
6. **Reserved matters** (shareholder and board level) — short list, §2.3. **Insist:** a mirror list where the founder's consent is required (changing the articles, issuing shares outside the pool, selling the company or key IP).
7. **Share issues and pre-emption**; option pool size and process.
8. **Transfer restrictions:** lock-up for the founder (accept 2–3 years with exceptions for a holding company and estate planning); right of first refusal (on top of the articles' redemption clause); permitted transfers.
9. **Drag-along and tag-along** (§2.6).
10. **Vesting and leaver terms** for founder and key employees (§2.4).
11. **Liquidation preference** and the waterfall for sale proceeds (§2.8).
12. **Anti-dilution** (§2.7).
13. **Information rights** and reporting; confidentiality (no personal data of users beyond what is lawful).
14. **Non-compete and non-solicitation** for shareholders working in the company (reasonable in time, place and scope; a non-compete with employees is governed by the Employment Contracts Act, see 05).
15. **IP:** confirmation that all IP is owned by the company (03), and that future IP of working shareholders goes to it.
16. **Deadlock:** escalation, then mediation; avoid "shotgun" clauses that a better-funded investor can use against the founder.
17. **Breach:** contractual penalty (sopimussakko) amounts that are proportionate; a court may adjust unreasonable terms (Contracts Act 228/1929 §36; not re-read here).
18. **Term:** until an IPO or until a party holds no shares.
19. **Accession:** new shareholders and option holders who exercise must sign an accession deed.
20. **Notices, governing law, disputes** (Finland Chamber of Commerce arbitration or district court).

**Clauses the founder should insist on:** founder consent for structural changes; founder-majority board while A-share control lasts; narrow bad-leaver; double-trigger acceleration; a price floor and the founder in any drag threshold; the right to transfer shares to a founder holding company; reasonable non-compete limits.

## 5. Red flags

- An investor wants control (board majority, managing-director choice, budget veto) at pre-seed or seed.
- Full-ratchet anti-dilution, participating or multiple preferences early.
- A personal guarantee or personal liability of the founder for company obligations.
- A redemption / put right that lets investors demand their money back.
- "Advisers" asking for equity for introductions, or a fee to "access investors" (success fees on fundraising may need regulatory checks; ask the lawyer).
- Pressure to sign quickly, to skip a lawyer, or to accept documents only in a language you do not read.
- A term sheet with exclusivity before any real terms are agreed.
- An investor refusing to sign any confidentiality terms **and** asking for the source code (VCs commonly refuse NDAs; then share metrics and demos, not code or personal data).
- A request to put user personal data in the data room (GDPR: share aggregated data only).
- Anyone promising "€400 million" quickly: see the realism notes in `docs/business/fundraising-plan.md`.
- Valuation caps or convertibles where the founder cannot calculate the dilution: ask the accountant for a cap-table model before signing.
